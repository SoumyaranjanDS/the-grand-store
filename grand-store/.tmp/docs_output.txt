Yes. If you want Grand Store to operate a professional, high-trust auction platform for rare and exclusive whisky, I would build it more like a specialist auction house + secure marketplace, rather than simply adding an “Auction” button to an e-commerce site.
For South Africa in particular, online auctions are subject to the Consumer Protection Act (CPA), its auction regulations, and electronic-commerce requirements. The regulations specifically address internet auctions, bidder registration, security, records and auction rules.

2. The most important principle: don't allow anyone to bid immediately
For expensive whisky, registration should not equal bidding approval.
I would create:
REGISTERED → KYC CHECK → AGE CHECK → PAYMENT VERIFICATION → RISK CHECK → APPROVED BIDDER
Only then does the "Place Bid" button become active.
South African online-auction regulations require prospective internet bidders to register with information including their full name, ID/passport number, age, physical address, IP address and payment method. 
So your developer should build a proper Bidder Verification Engine, not merely a normal customer registration.

10. Auction closing — use an anti-sniping rule
This is one of the biggest protections I would recommend.
Suppose an auction closes at:
20:00

19:59:59

Last-minute bid detected. Auction extended by 2 minutes.

Extended again by 2 minutes.
This prevents someone from winning purely because they submitted a bid milliseconds before closing.
You can call this:
"Fair Close / Dynamic Extension"
