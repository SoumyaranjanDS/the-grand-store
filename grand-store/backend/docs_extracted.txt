Yes. I would make the checkout specification very structured, because this is one of the areas where a small mistake in tax, shipping, currency or duties can create accounting and customer-service problems.

One important correction first: 15% VAT should not simply be applied to every Grand Store sale in every circumstance. SARS currently states that the standard VAT rate is 15%, but certain supplies are zero-rated or exempt, and export treatment has specific rules. The system should therefore determine VAT from the vendor's VAT status, product/tax classification, seller location, customer location and delivery/export circumstances, rather than hard-coding "15% on everything."

VENDOR SETTLEMENT

The most important instruction to your developer

The checkout must have one single source of truth for the final amount. The customer must never see one amount on the product page, another amount at checkout and another amount at payment. The system must calculate product price, applicable VAT/taxes, shipping, courier charges, international duties/taxes, discounts and currency conversion before payment, validate everything again immediately before payment, and store the exact calculation used for accounting and auditing.

And for international orders:

Never tell the customer that "shipping is €120" and leave it at that. Clearly identify whether duties, taxes, customs clearance and other import charges are included, estimated or payable separately.

That approach gives Grand Store a checkout that is simple for the customer but sophisticated enough behind the scenes to handle South African sales, PostNet collection, domestic courier delivery, international vendors, France → Dubai shipments, live courier APIs, VAT, customs, duties, currencies and multi-vendor orders without having to redesign the checkout later.
